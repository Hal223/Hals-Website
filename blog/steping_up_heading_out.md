
# The Quiet Gap of Leadership
`2025-08-15`
![[hurting.jpg]]

There is a quiet truth that travels through every family and community: anything worth having is worth having. When the promise of something good sits in our hands, we feel compelled to guard it, to nurture it, and to make sure it does not slip away.

Evil does not come from darkness itself; it rises when good men do nothing. I heard a story that has lingered with me. A family well-structured, one might have compared them to A TV archetype was rocked by the loss of their father. Their grandfather stepped in, a steady hand and a calm voice, and the family held together. Then he died too. Followed was a decay of the family, the bonds frayed, with the loss of a leader petty gossip and tribal rivalries arose.

A loss occurred in my own family, I started to notice the decay, I made a conscious choice to step up and lead. I spoke only of fairness and respect; I did not preach or dictate. I worked unbelievably well, the arguments stopped, moments to cherish followed. The family listened, and their voices softened. It seemed a simple act of order and reason had rekindled something fragile.

Then fatigue set in. I pulled back from being the one to call out missteps, and the old pattern returned. A quiet lesson emerged: leadership is not a permanent position but a shared responsibility that must be accepted and earned. When we ignore to hold onto it, the light fades for everyone else.

The world outside my family mirrors this pattern. In our local institutions schools, churches, businesses there is a gap in leadership. Many people are content to wait for someone else to take charge. They lack the inner drive that turns an ordinary day into something extraordinary. When a leader steps forward, even briefly, they can ignite a spark that transforms conflict into cooperation.

History repeats itself in waves. Hard times forge strong men. Those strong men build good times. Good times breed complacency. Complacency creates weak men who are unprepared for the next wave of hardship. The cycle is inevitable unless we intervene.

I do not intend to preaching a doctrine, merely share an observation: The gap between good men doing nothing and the rise of darkness can be bridged by one act of quiet courage. If we each take that step, even in the smallest ways, the collective light will shine brighter. And when it does, perhaps the next wave of hard times will meet a community ready to greet it with strength.
